## Engineering answer

Strong on-axis support is a positional measurement, not a stroke-identity measurement. Distinct local strokes can occupy the same fitted corridor, near-collinear pieces can change lateral level, and crossings or terminal foreign strokes can contribute ink without preserving one local trajectory. The bounded evidence therefore separates full-span coherence from endpoint integrity; one generic scalar is not sufficient.

This audit implements deterministic profiles only. It does not compose a new guard into DEV eligibility because the reviewed set mixes primitive defects with candidate-level semantic/admissibility failures, while several endpoint signals remain ambiguous at legitimate Hough truncations and crossings. Existing span, axis, and admissibility outcomes are unchanged.

## Bounded evidence set

Reviewed 19 unique Batch-02 cases: all 17 axis-supported cases (the 8 axis-supported human-NOT_DIRECT cases plus all 9 human-direct controls) and 2 noisy/degraded axis-uncertain comparison controls. No fresh candidates were mined, no Batch-03 was created, and no model was run.

For every raw Hough ancestor, the audit records normalized local center offset, local tangent delta, source-associated width, bounded transition continuity, multi-peak/junction evidence, and both endpoint neighborhoods. Thresholds are normalized by the locally estimated stroke width where displacement is involved.

## Findings

The measurements show two independent layers. Full-span failures are expressed by sustained lateral level changes or material disjoint intervals. Endpoint failures are expressed by a terminal offset/width/branch transition or a supported tail after an internal termination. A legitimate continuous line can cross a perpendicular line or a local symbol without losing its core trajectory, so crossing evidence alone never rejects.

In the 19 real reviewed cases, coherence produced 9 supported, 10 uncertain, and 0 rejected decisions. Endpoint analysis produced 2 rejected overshoot diagnostics and 17 uncertain decisions, with 0 generically supported at candidate-fragment level because ordinary Hough truncation commonly continues into nearby axial ink. The synthetic suite proves measurement sensitivity, but the real-control overlap prevents either diagnostic from becoming a safe runtime guard.

Axis-supported human-NOT_DIRECT records whose raw primitives remain coherent are classified as candidate/semantic failures rather than being forced into a primitive defect. Conversely, a human-DIRECT record may carry an endpoint-quality concern without changing its continuation label.

The known thin-hollow centerline family is not re-solved here: it remains owned by the preceding axis-integrity guard and is outside the selected axis-supported primary set.

## Engineering decision

A. Remaining failures: **MIXED** — primitive-coherence, endpoint-quality, and candidate/semantic mechanisms are all present or plausible and cannot safely share one verdict.

B. One generic coherence guard justified: **NO**. The diagnostic is useful, but this small reviewed set does not establish a safe rejection boundary across legitimate long lines, crossings, thick strokes, and scan wobble.

C. Separate endpoint guard justified: **NO** for runtime rejection. Endpoint evidence must remain distinct, but legitimate Hough truncation and crossings make the current generic signal appropriate for audit/UNCERTAIN use only.

D. Batch-03 resume immediately: **NO**. Resolve the mixed primitive/candidate meaning and collect targeted endpoint/coherence controls first.

E. Model bake-off resume: **NO**. Dataset meaning is not clean enough.
